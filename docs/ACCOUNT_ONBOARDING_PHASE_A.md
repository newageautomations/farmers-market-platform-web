# Account onboarding Phase A

## Executive summary

The platform already provisions personal accounts and initial business roles. The requested onboarding would start after provisioning and guide people through the existing product. Keep business progress independent for every `Vendor.id` and independently managed `Market.id`, and keep customer progress on the signed-in platform account. A user can be a customer, an owner in one workspace, and an employee in another. Their current role, channel, and permissions determine what they can do in each workspace.

Several useful milestones already have durable evidence, including an assigned profile image, a classified product and priced variant, a published website version, a submitted application, a scheduled occurrence, a published map snapshot, and an issued invoice. Provisioning defaults, provider verification, and changing readiness require different treatment. A first vendor or market can be created before configuring a storefront or payment provider. Customer shopping and checkout can proceed without a registered account.

This review used current local frontend and sibling backend source plus project documentation. It includes the in-progress listing approvals and application/assignment changes present in the local worktree. **Implemented** means a reachable frontend workflow and corresponding live-code backend path were found. **Partial** indicates a restricted, hidden, environment-dependent, or incomplete path as described. **Placeholder** means explicit UI with no matching capability at that entry. **Proposed** means future work. Runtime behavior, deployed flags, provider access, and populated database contents were outside this source-only review. Some historical reports describe behavior since superseded in source.

### IDs and authority

- **Customer.** Use the canonical platform `User.id` for one person's account progress across storefronts. Resolve it from the authenticated customer profile/session and its linked `Customer` for commerce evidence. Do not key by email, phone, guest `CheckoutContact`, storefront hostname, order ID, or a merchant's CRM relationship. Establish how any future canonical identity merge carries progress.
- **Vendor and manager.** Use a typed entity key of `VENDOR` plus `Vendor.id` or `MARKET` plus `Market.id`. Vendor `primaryChannelId` and Market `channelId` are execution and authorization context. `Storefront.id`, `liveWebsiteId`, Slug, `Seller.id`, and provider account ID represent narrower resources or external mappings. `TenantMembership.id` is suitable for a staff-specific acknowledgement if needed, with the principal and workspace rechecked on access. `PlatformRoleOnboarding.subjectId` is useful provenance for self-serve creation and covers only its one initial subject per role.
- **Enforcement.** `TenantAccessPolicy`, `business-api-boundary.ts`, managed native roles and exact channel grants form the backend boundary. `TenantMembership` connects the native principal to a specific active vendor or market. `BusinessOwnership` identifies protected ownership. `BusinessTeamService` initializes managed owner and employee roles and limits delegation to the business capability allowlist. Employees can hold distinct administrator, lead, or staff capabilities. Provider ownership, legal identity, banking, ownership transfer and business deletion have protected boundaries. Preserve each business command's owner, permission, entitlement, resource, revision and provider checks. Frontend `routeAllowed` and `mayCommand` are presentation gates. Platform provisioning and review are separate trusted authority.
- **Account and checkout isolation.** Central password login, merchant customer sessions, legacy email/claim flows and Admin use separate session purposes and bounded handoffs. The checkout coordinator and purchase claims determine order/guest ownership. Signup can use sealed contact prefill from a committed purchase and return to `/account/orders` through a merchant handoff. Landing in the guide must be lower priority than payment recovery, a checkout/sign-in return, application draft/claim, invitation, or an explicit workspace selection.

### Progress semantics

- Derive current evidence on entry and after returning from a linked workflow. Evaluate from authoritative persisted data, and use existing durable facts where their meaning matches. A backend or authorization failure is an unknown/unavailable state. Older data can establish current configuration without proving when or by whom a task was intentionally reviewed.
- Plan a durable historical progress record only where needed to retain completion, explicit decisions, skipped steps, education acknowledgements, and resume position. Associate evidence with a typed subject, stable step key, evidence kind and time. Store only the minimal reference needed, especially for private documents, customer data and provider records. Events can invalidate or refresh cached projections. A bounded scoped read on resume covers work done elsewhere and legacy events that do not exist.
- Keep “completed once” and “ready now” independent. For example, preserve completion of a published website or connected account after later removal or provider restriction. Explain current issues with the established workflow links. Refresh evidence after writes and stale responses, and never infer authoritative completion from optimistic UI, a return URL, local storage, or a successful HTTP response alone.
- Treat applicability, permission, and waiting as distinct states. A blocked staff action can point to the owner, and a pending external verification can remain pending while the rest of the guide is used. Optional skips retain their scope and reason. Define how chapter totals treat unavailable and externally blocked steps before implementation.

## Architectural risks, limitations and unresolved decisions

- **Same-role provisioning scope.** The product requirement to support onboarding per business is compatible with current trusted multiple affiliations. True repeat self-service creation needs a deliberate separate provisioning design, including identity, duplicate policy and limits. Preserve the existing initial activation row and its role preference eligibility semantics.
- **Permission granularity.** Decide which historical status is appropriate for employees who may see the workspace but cannot read related documents, finance or payment records. Server-side summaries should reveal only information the caller is entitled to see. Use protected business owner checks even when the owner originally created the progress record.
- **Defaults, imports and historical ambiguity.** Decide for each step whether existing valid data proves satisfaction, or whether the requested action is intentional review. Starter content, automatically created draft invoices and purchase-generated follows need explicitly different treatment. Read-only source inspection cannot establish the shape of all legacy rows.
- **Provider and environment capability.** Stripe Connect current readiness source restricts activation to test mode, with collection enabled by the separate configured runtime path. Some POS provider entries are hidden, the vendor fallback checkout has placeholders, and website analytics is explicitly planned. Vendor booth invoices are payable through the market invoice workflow. An independent vendor accounts-receivable invoice builder and a general vendor business contact editor were not verified. These are not required guide chapters.
- **Completion policy.** Decide the scope of staff acknowledgements, what “completed” means when branches change, how deleted resources are handled, and which durable facts can be read efficiently with current indexes/projections before introducing new persistence. Separate historical semantics from operational reporting and notification policy.
- **Availability and privacy.** A guide is another tenant-sensitive read surface. Consider stale cached workspace identity, simultaneous browser tabs, revoked grants, provider callback retries, guest claim binding, and the exact authorization of referenced records. Prefer minimal completion predicates over returning raw private data. Use bounded queries and avoid per-record calls for a chapter list.
- **Rollout and local changes.** This inventory includes current uncommitted changes to listing approvals and application/assignment views. Recheck their final routes and deployment status before binding the future guide. Current local code establishes integration points, while deployed settings, provider behavior, data backfill results and browser appearance remain unverified.

## Recommended implementation phases

1. **Phase B, requirements and architecture review.** Confirm role boundaries, same-kind provisioning scope, chapter applicability, stable milestone IDs, staff visibility, completion semantics and legacy adoption. Specify read/write contracts and persistence needs with actual evidence and queries. Keep purpose, destination and entitlement decisions explicit.
2. **Progress foundation and narrow vertical slice.** After approval, implement tenant-safe progress/read projections, auditable manual acknowledgement where needed, scoped navigation and one end-to-end chapter per role. Verify cross-tenant denial, revocation, permission-dependent actions, resume across devices, an independently completed task, and unchanged guest/claim/invitation flows.
3. **Operational chapters.** Extend verified detectors and authorized redirects to catalog, schedules, layouts, applications, websites and payment readiness. Include optional branches, external waiting, version changes, and recovery when a workflow or provider is unavailable. Keep business writes in their established services.
4. **Adoption and release qualification.** Backfill only well-defined evidence idempotently, expose legacy accounts to the guide without blocking ordinary use, and qualify existing and new states on guarded disposable data before any requested persistent test migration. Verify accessibility, responsive navigation, context switches, checkout and provider boundaries under separately authorized test/release work.

Phase A ends with this local report. Application code, account data, schema, configuration and existing documentation were left untouched. No server, suite or browser was started for this investigation.
