## 1. Executive summary

**Current recommended design:** Preserve one shopping cart and customer order, with a durable coordination group and one connected-account direct charge and real Vendure payment per participating vendor. Authorize all eligible card payments before coordinated capture and place the purchase only after every obligation settles. Collect only the platform component as an application fee. Refund customer principal from the original connected account and return refundable application fees through independently reconciled operations. Prefer a custom direct-charge payment adapter for existing local booth invoices. See the approved-direction section for the complete design, implementation gates and unresolved approvals.

**Verified correction:** Market checkout uses one customer aggregate order and one settled customer payment, followed by a seller order per vendor and detailed original-unit allocations. Multi-vendor settlement and refunds are launch gates. Vendor storefront checkout is currently a non-paying preview.

## 5. Recommended Stripe Connect architecture

**Historical, superseded:** The charge recommendations in this section and the flows in sections 6 through 10 are retained as audit history only. Use the approved-direction section below for all implementation decisions.

| Scenario | Recommendation | Consequences |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single-vendor preorder | Platform destination charge with `transfer_data.destination` and `application_fee_amount` | One recipient; platform pays Stripe processing fees and bears platform charge refunds/disputes. |
| Multi-vendor preorder | One platform charge and separate transfers to each vendor | Platform retains the difference as the economic equivalent of a fee, **not** an application-fee object. Platform owns allocation, transfer timing and recovery. |
| Booth invoice | Provisional platform Stripe Invoice with destination and application fee, using Hosted Invoice Page | Platform customer and charge, one market recipient; validate issuer, invoice compatibility and Invoicing fees. |
| Future POS | Consider Terminal direct charges on the vendor account and application fees | Distinct card-present pricing, reader, merchant, capability and refund obligations; retain existing POS methods. |

Direct charges reside on the connected account and can collect application fees. Fee and loss responsibility depend on the account configuration and commercial terms. They may better fit a connected seller as merchant, but require connected-account context and appropriate customer ownership. Destination charges simplify one-recipient checkout while centralizing exposure. Separate charges and transfers support multiple recipients but are not an atomic split and do not automatically reverse transfers on refund. See [Stripe charge types](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/charges), [direct charges](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/direct-charges), [destination charges](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/destination-charges), [separate charges and transfers](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/separate-charges-and-transfers), and [Terminal with Connect](https://docs.stripe.com/terminal/features/connect).

Stripe-hosted onboarding minimizes sensitive data collection. Accounts v2 supports merchant, recipient and customer configurations; Stripe documents v1 API compatibility for some v2 accounts. Confirm the selected account, event, invoice and Dashboard features before adopting it. Legacy Standard/Express/Custom names alone do not settle fee, loss, requirements or Dashboard responsibilities. Dashboard choice may be permanent. Compare hosted with embedded onboarding, and request only needed capabilities. [Accounts v2](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/accounts-v2), [hosted onboarding](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/hosted-onboarding), [capabilities](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/account-capabilities).

Payment Element is a good fit for the existing authoritative checkout. Stripe Checkout hosted is an alternative if its line items, tax, loyalty, discounts and pickup can reproduce the frozen local quote. Limit methods to those individually qualified, especially where settlement is asynchronous. A redirect or client confirmation is not proof of payment.

Destination charges are platform charges. `on_behalf_of` can affect settlement and statement behavior but does not independently resolve legal merchant-of-record or tax obligations. Stripe's invoice guide describes automatic-transfer invoices as platform business of record. Obtain counsel and Stripe confirmation of legal seller, issuer, liability and any `on_behalf_of` use. [Connect invoices](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/invoices).

## 6. Detailed preorder payment flow and funds movement

1. Server validates cart, seller, stock, contact, pickup, discounts, loyalty and taxes; freezes a versioned quote and recipient allocations. Reject mixed currency, unsupported paths and recipients lacking required capability.
2. Create or recover one idempotent payment attempt and platform PaymentIntent with exact server total and destination. Return a client secret only to the authorized checkout session. Display subtotal, discounts, tax, shipping/pickup, separate Preorder fee and final total before confirmation.
3. Stripe.js handles confirmation and customer authentication. Verified events and authoritative Stripe retrieval drive the existing coordinator and Vendure payment/order transition. Pending, failed, delayed and uncertain states remain visible. Define recovery for a successful payment arriving after inventory hold expiry.
4. For total `T = B + F`, where B is the vendor entitlement and F the collected fee, a destination charge transfers funds to the connected account and the application fee returns to the platform. Vendor proceeds are B before any connected-account-specific charges. Stripe deducts actual processing cost C from the platform; the platform's contribution is `F - C`, not F as pure profit. Availability in a Stripe balance is not a bank payout.
5. For multiple vendors freeze `B_i` with `sum(B_i) = B`, including deterministic allocations of discounts, tax and pickup. After eligible successful collection, create idempotent transfers per recipient; the platform retains F and pays C. Persist each transfer and pending/failure status. Wait for confirmed success for asynchronous methods and follow method-specific Stripe guidance. Transfer group links do not make separate transfers atomic.

**Assumptions requiring confirmation:** B includes the correct seller entitlement and tax/pickup treatment. Platform-funded loyalty or promotions may require a subsidy. Define zero-total orders, unavailable sellers, multiple markets, mixed currency and late settlement explicitly. Do not launch multi-vendor payments before allocation, refund and transfer-recovery qualification.

## 7. Detailed booth invoice payment flow and funds movement

1. Preserve automatically generated local drafts and offline payments. When online billing is selected, freeze local invoice revision, recipient, line amounts, currency, tax, due date and fee. Bind the recipient Stripe account to the market organization.
2. Create or recover a platform Stripe Customer and draft Stripe Invoice, with matching lines and a separate Service fee line, `transfer_data.destination` for the market and `application_fee_amount = F`. Compare exact frozen totals before finalization. Coordinate crash recovery and idempotency across both systems. Stripe supports Connect invoices and fixed application fees: [invoice creation](https://docs.stripe.com/api/invoices/create), [Connect invoices](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/invoices).
3. Present the Hosted Invoice Page through authorized vendor invoice access or an explicitly approved delivery path. Choose one email strategy, verify invoice branding and recipient access, and account for Stripe Invoicing charges. A custom Payment Element against the local invoice is an alternative if invoice identity, partial payments or cost make mirroring unsuitable; it requires more payment and reconciliation work.
4. Verified invoice payment events and object retrieval produce an idempotent local provider payment linked to the frozen invoice. Market receives B, the application fee F reaches the platform, and Stripe deducts actual processing cost C from the platform. Payment is not payout. Credits, voids and refunds require separate mappings and a credit note does not itself prove a completed refund.
5. Serialize manual coverage, online payment initiation, void and replacement using revision and durable operation state. A database lock cannot lock Stripe: recover unknown external outcomes before allowing another collection. Decide whether initial online payments are full-balance only, and how fees work after partial offline coverage. External businesses must have secure invoice access without being made fake platform vendors.

## 8. Platform fee calculation and accounting model

Use integer minor units and integer rational rates with overflow checks. Store versioned, server-controlled configuration per transaction type, currency, effective period and relevant jurisdiction: fixed/percentage platform pricing and separately configured processing estimate. Only authorized platform administrators can publish changes; retain actor and effective date. Freeze the configuration and all inputs for completed transactions. Rate changes affect future quotes only; changes to an already presented quote require expiry and renewed acceptance.

Let B be the approved post-discount base including whatever taxes and shipping policy requires; P the rounded platform fee; E the estimated processing component; F = P + E; and T = B + F. For an illustrative linear processing estimate `r*T + c`, calculate the smallest integer E satisfying `E >= ceil(r*(B + P + E) + c)`. Equivalently use the integer ceiling of `(r*(B + P) + c)/(1-r)` and verify the inequality using exact rational arithmetic. Require `0 <= r < 1`, safe bounds and explicit rounding. No rate is proposed. Fee taxation, caps, tiered or method-specific charges make this simple formula insufficient; use an approved bounded calculation and reject unsupported configurations. Decide whether platform percentage includes tax, pickup, discounts and loyalty and who funds each.

Snapshot base, tax, discount, allocation, fee, rates, formula version, rounding and total. Record actual C from Stripe balance-transaction fee details when available and record additional Invoicing, Connect, dispute or FX fees separately. Unknown is not zero. `F - C` is a contribution measure, not automatically revenue or net profit; an accountant must determine revenue recognition and tax treatment. Reconcile vendor payable, collected fees, actual costs, refunds, transfers, reversals and payouts as distinct events.

**Blocking compliance decision:** A fee applied independently of electronic payment method is not automatically lawful because it is named a service fee. Channel, offline treatment, economic purpose, disclosures, jurisdiction, network rules, Stripe agreement and taxes matter. Card surcharges have distinct restrictions, including debit/prepaid restrictions. Counsel and Stripe must review the exact fee and displays before launch. See [Stripe surcharge guidance](https://stripe.com/resources/more/surcharge-fees) and [convenience fee guidance](https://stripe.com/en-mt/resources/more/convenience-fees).

## 9. Connected-account onboarding and authorization

Associate each connection with a canonical vendor or market organization, platform account, Stripe account ID and test/live mode, with lifecycle history and scoped uniqueness. A user can belong to many workspaces; a browser-supplied account ID never establishes ownership. Decide explicitly whether the same legal entity may share an account across organizations.

Require owner or a specifically granted payment-management privilege on the server for connection, continuation, account management and disconnection. Recheck membership and organization on each request; audit actor and action. Use short-lived hosted onboarding links, server-controlled refresh/return URLs and a fresh Stripe read after return. Return alone does not establish readiness. Track requirements, transfer/card capabilities and payout status separately; support restricted and interrupted accounts. Webhooks and periodic retrieval update status. Block new online payments or transfers when the applicable capability is unavailable, and provide remediation. Do not silently replace an account with pending payments, refunds or balances. [Stripe verification guidance](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/handling-api-verification).

## 10. Refunds, disputes, webhooks, and reconciliation

Cancellation, invoice void, credit note and provider refund are separate facts. Freeze refund allocations in original minor units by seller, item, tax and fee; cap against remaining settled amounts. Persist pending/succeeded/failed provider operations and use stable idempotency keys. Only successful refunds affect settled refund reporting. Retrieve uncertain operations before retry.

Destination refunds draw on the platform; explicitly govern and track transfer reversal and application-fee refund options. For separate transfers, independently reverse eligible transfers and track failed reversals and unrecovered receivables. Connected balances may be insufficient, especially after payout; fund customer refunds independently rather than assuming recovery. Stripe says original processing fees generally are not returned and insufficient available balance may leave refunds pending. [Refunds](https://docs.stripe.com/refunds), [transfer reversals](https://docs.stripe.com/api/transfer_reversals). Decide full/partial fee refunds, seller cancellation treatment and who absorbs costs and losses. Handle disputes with evidence deadlines, an accountable operator and auditable provisional/final records; prevent overlap with refunds.

Register platform and connected-account event destinations appropriate to chosen v1/v2 APIs. Verify the signature over raw bytes with endpoint-specific rotating secrets; validate mode, account and object ownership. Durably store the event and unique context before acknowledging, then process through the existing worker if its durability is verified. A transactional outbox or inbox poller closes the crash gap. Deduplicate both event delivery and business effects; fetch authoritative objects when events arrive out of order, apply guarded transitions and model later refund/dispute as separate events. Monitor dead letters, aged holds, failed transfers and refunds. [Stripe webhooks](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks).

Schedule reconciliation of PaymentIntents, charges, invoice payments, application fees, transfers, reversals, refunds, disputes, balance transactions and payouts against local attempts and ledger, scoped by mode/account/currency. Use durable cursors and overlapping windows, detect Dashboard-originated changes, and queue discrepancies for authorized review. Keep unknown fees pending. Separate test/live credentials, endpoint secrets and mappings; pin API versions and prevent secret exposure in browser bundles or logs.

### Existing order, payment and fulfillment model

**Verified:** For a market occurrence, one customer checkout starts on the customer order, becomes a Vendure `Aggregate` order, and is split after payment into seller orders grouped by vendor primary channel. `ProtectedOrderSellerStrategy.splitOrder()` in `src/plugins/marketplace-commerce/commerce/strategies.ts` groups customer lines and their owned shipping lines, while `NativeCommerceAdapter.afterSplit()` and `link()` in `commerce/native-commerce.adapter.ts` create seller `OrderContext` and `OrderLineLink` records linking original and operational orders/lines, vendor, channel, variant, quantity, inventory source and checkout attempt. A direct-vendor or in-person context returns no seller split. Seller lifecycle requires a committed attempt and exactly one settled payment on the aggregate parent, per `sellerOperationalEligibility()` in `strategies.ts`. This is one successful purchase payment and snapshot, not one payment per seller. Failed/retried attempts may exist over the lifetime of a cart; do not interpret this as a promise that an order can never have historical attempts.

`CheckoutCoordinator.begin/finalize/release` in `commerce/checkout-coordinator.ts`, capacity and hold services, and `NativeCommerceAdapter.commit` protect placement. `src/plugins/marketplace-commerce/financial/financial-attribution.service.ts` requires a settled single payment equal to the order total, a matching successful capture fact, complete line links and one operational portion per vendor. Preserve frozen quote, customer identity, stock/capacity, occurrence context, pickup ownership, seller-channel isolation and atomic local placement. Do not treat a seller order as an independently charged order. `inventory/inventory-coordinator.ts` delegates fulfillment and unfulfilled-quantity cancellation to operational writers; `strategies.ts` supports seller partial-delivery state transitions. Exact eligibility and all interaction between cancellation, fulfillment and refunds require scenario-level qualification before launch.

**Verified money allocation:** `FinancialAttributionService.snapshot()` records `PurchaseFinancialSnapshot`, vendor, line and original-unit allocations from `financial/entities.ts`. Line merchandise gross, discount and item tax derive from Vendure prices; vendor-owned shipping net and tax are allocated to covered lines in proportion to discounted merchandise, then split over original units. `MoneyAllocator` in `financial/money-allocator.ts` uses integer/BigInt arithmetic and deterministic largest remainder with stable keys. It rejects unsupported inclusive or adjusted shipping and shipping without a method. ### Proposed fee refund policy and financial examples

**Recommended default, not approved or implemented:** refund the proportional originally allocated Preorder fee and any tax on that fee when the associated eligible items are refunded, for vendor-caused and customer-requested refunds alike; return the entire remaining fee on total cancellation. Allocate the fee in cents to vendor and then original units using a documented stable largest-remainder rule at purchase, preserving separate processing estimate and platform components even though the customer sees one label. Maintain cumulative reservations and refunded totals per unit/component; final refunds consume residual cents so cumulative fee refunds cannot exceed the originally collected amount. Do not recalculate historic rates. Platform initially absorbs actual nonrefundable Stripe cost and records a contractual receivable or loss only if the approved agreement allows it. Counsel and Stripe must approve this policy, fee character, fee-tax and disclosures. A different cause-based policy needs explicit owner approval and legal review.

**Example assumptions:** Each example is independent. Customer paid $65 merchandise ($30 A, $20 B, $15 C) plus $4 fee, total $69. No tax, shipping, discounts or loyalty in these illustrative amounts. Largest remainder apportions the $4 by merchandise value: A $1.85, B $1.23, C $0.92. Each vendor was transferred its full merchandise share, the platform retained $4 before an unknown actual processing cost C, and no transfer reversal has yet happened. These balances are transaction-specific Stripe-connected-account proceeds, not necessarily bank balances. The model assumes fee shares are platform-funded; connected vendors do not owe those fee shares unless separately agreed.

| Case | Customer refund and financial movement | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A: B cancels | Refund $21.23 ($20 B plus $1.23 allocated fee). Independently reverse B's $20 transfer if available. | A retains $30, B $0, C $15. Platform fee retained $2.77, contribution $2.77 − C, excluding other fees. |
| B: A refunds $10 | If the chosen original A units have $10 paid value and their frozen fee share is $0.62, refund $10.62 and reverse $10 of A's transfer. $0.62 is an example frozen allocation, not a general re-rounding rule. | A retains $20, B $20, C $15. Platform retains $3.38 before C. Limit to unrefunded units, original payment and remaining fee shares. | C: all cancel | Refund $69 including all $4 fee; separately reverse $30, $20 and $15 transfers. | All vendors retain $0 for this purchase; platform fee retained $0 and bears C and any other nonrefunded cost. All original units and fee shares have settled refund records. If previous partial refunds exist, refund only remaining balances. |
| D: B already paid out | Submit the $21.23 refund under the assumed fee policy and independently attempt $20 reversal. If B has insufficient available Stripe balance, reversal may fail; preserve a $20 recovery receivable/exception rather than marking it recovered. | A and C retain $30/$15; B has already received $20 pending recovery. In D, Stripe refunds use the platform's available balance. Maintain a funded reserve/top-up and monitor pending/failed refunds; a customer refund cannot be guaranteed immediately if that balance is insufficient. Attempt a permissible reversal later, offset a future payable only if legally and contractually permitted, seek repayment or write off after review. Stripe limits reversals to the unreversed transfer amount and available connected balance may constrain them. [Stripe refunds](https://docs.stripe.com/refunds), [transfer reversal API](https://docs.stripe.com/api/transfer_reversals/create). For disputes, the platform balance is debited on indirect charges, including with `on_behalf_of`; track associated recovery, evidence and deadlines separately ([Connect disputes](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/disputes)).

### Parent order, coordination group and payment invariants

**Recommendation:** Keep multiple native `Payment` records on the existing customer parent. Add `CheckoutPaymentGroup` and `VendorPaymentObligation` as coordination structures, not a second cart/order system. Each positive obligation corresponds to one vendor's frozen total, one connected-account PaymentIntent and, after successful aggregate collection, exactly one native payment. The seller order remains an operational projection with `OrderLineLink` provenance. Regular direct-vendor orders use the same group with one obligation. POS/cash and historical purchases retain their existing supported path.

For vendor i, define `T_i = B_i + E_i + P_i + H_i`, where B includes allocated discounted merchandise, item tax, pickup/shipping and their tax; E is processing estimate, P platform fee, and H approved tax on the added fee. Then:

- The frozen parent total equals `sum(T_i)`. Its fee surcharges and tax must exist in the authoritative native quote, not merely a browser total.
- Every vendor/line/unit belongs to exactly one obligation; currency, vendor, account, mode and quote fingerprint match throughout. One account per vendor obligation does not permit accidentally combining two vendors into one charge.
- Every positive T has exactly one successful, unreversed capture fact with `amount_received = T_i`, one current native payment of `amount = T_i`, and one purchase-payment allocation. Successful capture evidence is distinct from authorization evidence.
- Placement requires all required obligations captured, complete immutable allocation coverage, matching total, valid identity/occurrence/pickup/promotions and valid inventory/capacity/loyalty holds. Any unknown or compensating obligation blocks placement and fulfillment.
- `sum(native Settled amounts) = frozen total` is necessary but insufficient. Reject duplicate, unrelated, mismatched, cross-account, refunded-before-placement or missing obligations even when sums match.
- Aggregate status is derived from obligations and placement facts. Post-purchase vendor refunds change financial/refund projections without pretending that the original captures never occurred or disabling unrelated vendor fulfillment.

**Vendure integration sequence:** Keep provider authorizations and capture results in obligations before placement. Do not attach native `Authorized` payments during this stage: complete authorization coverage can trigger native placement too early. After all captures are verified, enter the existing guarded capacity/placement transaction. Under the parent/group mutex, verify the frozen quote again, then call `addPaymentToOrder()` in stable obligation order. The handler receives an internal placement capability and obligation ID, verifies durable capture evidence, and returns that obligation's exact amount, `Settled`, real provider transaction ID and immutable routing metadata. It must reject an amount above the supplied remaining balance, duplicate consumption or public caller-supplied amounts. Intermediate records provide partial native coverage; only final exact coverage may transition the parent to `PaymentSettled` and split seller orders. The entire sequence, links, snapshot, stock conversion and outbox commits or rolls back together. No Stripe network calls occur inside that transaction.

Extend placement capability from one fact ID to the exact capture-fact set. `StripeVerifiedCheckout.fact/accept` and `VerifiedCheckoutPort` must represent group readiness, not manufacture one combined provider capture. Seller splitting hooks and notification subscribers must be transaction-aware: no fulfillment or success notification escapes an intermediate payment event. Verify native remaining-balance calculations and final transition behavior using the installed version. If this sequence cannot preserve native hooks, implement a plugin-scoped adapter using supported `PaymentService`/order transition APIs with equivalent checks; this is a phase-1 stop gate, not permission to write payment rows or force state directly.

Local placement failure rolls back all native payments and stock changes while preserving previously committed provider facts. Retry placement only while the exact quote and holds remain valid; otherwise compensate every captured obligation. Pre-placement compensation cannot require a `PurchaseFinancialSnapshot` or seller order that never committed. Use the frozen group allocation and provider-operation chain. A fresh attempt cannot reuse captured/uncertain obligations from an old group. Abort-and-compensate is irreversible for that group; a late success must join compensation, never resurrect purchase placement.

**Zero totals and unsupported pricing:** An entirely zero-payable purchase needs an explicit non-payment placement policy, not a synthetic Stripe settlement. Until separately qualified, reject zero-value vendor groups in paid multi-vendor checkout with an actionable cart message. Preserve current rejection of unsupported inclusive/adjusted shipping and unsupported promotions. Do not silently broaden eligibility. Mixed currency, unsupported account countries and unapproved subsidies fail before authorization. Platform-funded promotions or loyalty reimbursements need a distinct approved funding ledger; they cannot silently increase application fees or introduce transfers into this flow.

### Connected-account ownership, onboarding and liability

**Candidate configuration:** New Accounts v2 merchant accounts, `dashboard = full`, `defaults.responsibilities.fees_collector = stripe`, `defaults.responsibilities.losses_collector = stripe`. Set these deliberately at creation; responsibilities are immutable once merchant configuration is added. Stripe's documentation supports this responsibility model and says application fees should contain only the platform fee. This is technical suitability, conditional on actual platform eligibility and agreement. [Accounts v2 responsibilities](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/accounts-v2/connected-account-configuration), [integration recommendations](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/integration-recommendations).

Use the merchant `card_payments` and payout capabilities, supported currency/country and completed requirements as readiness evidence. A transfer-recipient capability is not a substitute for merchant readiness. Accounts v2 can be represented through compatible v1 endpoints for payments; preserve the account ID and normalize v1/v2 observations. Request required include-dependent fields explicitly rather than treating omitted responsibility data as approval. [Accounts v2 configurations](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/accounts-v2).

For **new accounts**, add Stripe-hosted onboarding with short-lived links and server-controlled return/refresh destinations. Verify the chosen account-link API and pinned SDK version against Accounts v2 during implementation; use documented compatible v1 endpoints if necessary. Link creation and continuation require current organization payment-management permission. Returning to the app triggers a server refresh, not a readiness grant. Full Dashboard access gives merchants their own refund/dispute operations. Do not adopt a preview Express/loss configuration as the default. [Hosted onboarding](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/hosted-onboarding).

For **existing OAuth connections**, retain and re-observe them. Confirm read/write scope, platform application, mode, connected-account identity, actual fee payer, loss responsibility, capabilities and access to charges/application fees. Do not recreate accounts or assume OAuth converts them to Accounts v2, changes their controller, or grants ownership of another app's fees. Existing-account connection remains distinct from new-account provisioning. If incompatible, block new paid checkout and require a supported Stripe-approved migration/reconnection path. Never mutate immutable responsibilities or replace an account automatically. [Existing-account OAuth guidance](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/standard-accounts), [OAuth reference](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/oauth-reference).

Persist the original connection generation on every obligation and refund. Refresh readiness at quote and again before capture, without rerouting an in-flight purchase. Explicitly distinguish new-charge readiness, payout readiness and permission to retrieve/refund historic charges. Restriction does not necessarily remove refund ability. Disconnect stops new obligations but keeps financial history, queued recovery and original identities. If authorization is revoked, pause affected operations and alert for vendor reconnection/Stripe-assisted resolution; never issue a platform charge to fund a substitute refund.

Payment-account management is organization-scoped, resolved through server membership and explicit read/manage/refund permissions. Bind OAuth/onboarding state to actor, organization, selected workspace, mode, nonce and expiry. A workspace switch invalidates pending UI requests and cannot retarget a callback; recheck permissions on completion. Proposed initial rule: one active account per organization/mode and no shared account across organizations without separately approved legal-entity ownership and permission design. Organization transfers and account replacements require audited handling of open obligations and refunds.

Stripe debits direct-charge refunds and disputed principal to the connected account. With Stripe assigned loss collection, the platform still bears its own negative balances and remains subject to its agreement, indemnities, prohibited activity and other obligations. Require written Stripe confirmation covering this actual marketplace business, territories, dispute fees, reserves, refunds after disconnection, and any exclusions. Do not market the model as a guarantee of zero platform liability. Vendor agreements should allocate refunds, chargebacks, processing expenses and balance funding to the merchant to the extent legally and contractually supported. [Connect balances](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/account-balances), [direct-charge disputes](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/disputes).

### Checkout lifecycle and aggregate recovery

Choose manual `capture_method=manual`, authorize all, then promptly capture exact frozen amounts. Do not hold card authorizations until market pickup. Compare each provider `capture_before` to the local deadline and leave a configured safety margin. This reduces compensation for authentication/decline failures; it does not make capture atomic. Immediate capture would charge early vendors before later vendors even authenticate and is rejected for version one. Both paths require compensating refunds; automatic delayed capture is unsuitable because it could capture an individual obligation after the group aborts. [Authorization/capture limits](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/place-a-hold-on-a-payment-method), [capture API](https://docs.stripe.com/api/payment_intents/capture).

| Stage/group state | Durable action, obligation state and exit condition |
| --- | --- |
| `QUOTED` | Compute server quote and component/unit allocations, validate seller/account readiness, occurrence rules, shipping/pickup, tax, supported promotions and loyalty. Return per-vendor T and combined total with fingerprint, expiry and disclosures. No money moves. |
| `RESERVED` | `CheckoutCoordinator.begin` acquires existing physical/offering-capacity and loyalty reservations, freezes identity and creates group/obligations atomically. Obligation `PLANNED`. Reject stale quote rather than charging an increased total. |
| `PREPARING` | Durable PI-create operation per obligation; create unconfirmed PI on original account for exact T with manual capture, explicit card method, `application_fee_amount=P`, group/obligation metadata. Persist account/mode and PI before returning secrets. Obligation `AWAITING_CONFIRMATION`. |
| `AUTHORIZING` | Customer confirms each PI; persist `REQUIRES_ACTION`, `AUTHORIZING` or `AUTHORIZED` from verified observations. `requires_capture` with full capturable T means authorized, not paid. Decline/abandonment stops capture and starts abort. Unexpected `processing` remains unresolved; no placement. |
| `READY_TO_CAPTURE` | All obligations authorized. Under group/parent lock recheck holds, quote/promotion eligibility, account readiness and capture deadlines; atomically record a fenced capture decision and bounded finalization deadline. |
| `CAPTURING` | Execute exact capture operations outside DB transactions, preferably stable sequential order for the first implementation. Stop new captures on confirmed failure. Each becomes `CAPTURED`, `CAPTURE_PENDING` or `CAPTURE_FAILED`; an unknown outcome is not failure. Preserve observed partial amounts as exceptions. |

| `PLACING` to `PLACED` | Existing capacity coordinator performs one transaction for genuine native payments, final `PaymentSettled`, vendor split, financial snapshot, stock allocation, loyalty consumption and outbox. Group placement evidence and attempt `COMMITTED` must agree. Dispatch one aggregate confirmation and vendor-specific order notifications only from committed outbox entries. |
| `ABORTING` / `COMPENSATING` | Permanently forbid placement. Cancel unconfirmed/authorized PIs; retrieve unknown captures; refund every successful capture in full including E/H and the entire P. Each cancellation/refund has its own durable operation. No vendor fulfillment or partial purchase success. |
| `ABORTED` | All PIs conclusively uncaptured/canceled and holds released. Customer can start a newly quoted attempt. |
| `COMPENSATED` | All required customer and fee refunds verified and reconciled, residual authorizations canceled, holds/loyalty released. Captures/refunds remain in the provider ledger even if no native order was placed. |
| `RECOVERY_REQUIRED` | Restricted access, pending insufficient-balance refunds, contradictory amounts or missed deadlines require monitored recovery. This is not a successful purchase or a completed refund. |

Store provider status separately from group decision and compensation status; this is not one linear enum that loses partial facts. Derive aggregate state deterministically, with abort/compensation taking precedence over capture progress and `PLACED` requiring its local commit fact. Post-placement statuses include per-vendor refund progress and aggregate `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`/`REFUNDED`; they do not imply a new checkout.

Unexpected partial capture or mismatched provider totals forbid placement and enter reconciliation/compensation. Refund only the verified actually captured residual and actual collected application fee, each net of existing refunds and reservations; never request the uncaptured part of T. Planned P is not proof of a collected fee. If P is zero, record a verified not-applicable fee leg rather than submit a zero-value application-fee refund. Amount or identity discrepancies remain visible even after customer reimbursement.

**Hold safety:** The inspected inventory coordinator expires `HELD` attempts and revalidates quote and time even after native allocation. Add a bounded payment-finalization lease recognized by hold expiry/release and recovery, acquired under the same parent/attempt mutex before capture. It must cover all stock, occurrence capacity, promotion usage and loyalty reservations, not just a group timestamp. Never keep SQL locks open across Stripe requests. A suggested initial customer-action budget is 10 minutes, capped by occurrence cutoff and existing policy; the owner must approve the actual TTL and finalization margin. No unbounded extension or heartbeat from an untrusted browser. On terminal abort or deadline, release sellable capacity once placement is irrevocably prohibited; continue financial recovery independently. Unknown late success after release is always refunded, never fulfilled using someone else's stock. Preserve exact native stock movement and offering commitment checks.

Promotion eligibility must be reserved or revalidated before capture and again during placement. Vendure's inspected add-payment path can remove an expired/usage-limited coupon. Any resulting change invalidates the frozen group; never charge a higher amount or change unit allocations after authorization. If this occurs after capture, roll back local placement and compensate. Preserve vendor loyalty's current rule: earned points reverse on settled returned units; spent points restore once all original units for that vendor are refunded. Market challenge refunds already key reversals to original unit refund allocations; keep that event path idempotent and qualify reward reconciliation. No points are earned or consumed for an unplaced compensated purchase.

**Idempotency and uncertainty:** Persist intent/hash before every provider mutation; key by group, obligation, operation kind and generation with immutable mode/account context. Use leases/fencing so a stale worker cannot initiate a new capture after abort. An already in-flight call may still succeed; sweeps must detect and compensate it. On restart recover the same operations, not another PI. Retry an identical Stripe request only within its documented idempotency window; after that retrieve/list by stored identity and metadata and reconcile before any new mutation. `find()` returning nothing once is not proof of no effect. Record proven no-effect failures separately from timeouts. Every group/operation remains sweepable even if optional queue wakeups fail.

Customer messages should distinguish: "Each vendor processes a separate payment"; "Confirm payment for [vendor]"; "Payments are authorized while we finish checkout"; "We are verifying your payment. Please do not pay again"; and "Checkout could not be completed. No pickup order was placed. Some payments are being refunded and temporary holds may remain." Show vendor amounts and actual pending/refunded statuses. Do not promise immediate refund arrival, hide partial financial success, or issue an order-ready notification during compensation.

### Fee computation, allocation and financial evidence

Use integer minor units and rational rates. Let B be the vendor's allocated payable base, P the configured platform fee computed once from an approved base with explicit rounding, r=n/d the estimated card percentage, f the per-charge fixed processing estimate, and E the processing recovery. With no fee tax and a conservative ceiling for the processing estimate:

```text
T = B + P + E
E = ceil((n * (B + P) + d * f) / (d - n))
estimatedProcessing(T) = ceil(n * T / d) + f
```

This is the smallest integer E satisfying `E >= estimatedProcessing(B+P+E)` under that versioned estimator. Require `0 <= n < d`; use BigInt ceiling division and checked conversion to supported native/provider integer ranges. Calculate per vendor charge so fixed costs are counted per payment. The example rate below is illustrative and is not a claim about an account's negotiated Stripe pricing.

If added-fee tax H depends on P/E, use a bounded monotone integer solver for the least E satisfying `E >= estimatedProcessing(B+P+E+H(P,E))`, with the approved tax engine and rounding contract. Reject unsupported or nonconverging configurations. Snapshot the final taxed native quote, not a client approximation. Legal/accounting must decide whether fee tax belongs to the vendor and stays in their account; the proposed application fee is exactly P, so platform-remitted tax cannot be silently folded into it. Cross-border/FX/Connect/Invoicing/dispute charges are separately recorded costs, not silently assumed covered by this estimate.

Freeze B/P/E/H, all rate numerators/denominators/fixed amounts, eligible bases, tax evidence, provider pricing assumption version, refund cause rules, quote expiry and disclosure version. Allocate P, E and each tax component to original units once with `MoneyAllocator` stable largest remainder, weighted by eligible discounted paid value; specify deterministic fallback weights for zero weights or reject the unsupported quote. Retain vendor pickup/shipping ownership and existing unit shares. Reconcile both component sums and `sum(T_i)` to the native order. Refunds select those cents; never recompute the original percentage using today's price or rate.

The customer sees one Preorder fee per vendor and a combined summary, with explanation that estimated processing costs may differ from actual costs. The connected account collects T; Stripe deducts actual processing C and platform application fee P. Platform receives P, while vendor transaction proceeds before tax remittance and other costs are `T - C - P = B + E + H - C`. Record E as recovery revenue/cost offset only as accounting approves. Actual provider fee observations must distinguish Stripe processing from application fees and fee taxes: `balance_transaction.fee` can include multiple fee types, so treating its total as C would double-count P. Unknown actual C remains unknown until retrieved. Payment settlement, Stripe balance availability and bank payout remain separate facts.

### Vendor refunds and exact application-fee refunds

**Route from original evidence:** Resolve purchase -> vendor allocation -> purchase-payment allocation -> original account/mode/charge/PI/application fee. Never use the vendor's current replacement account or the parent total. The customer's card refund is created with the platform credential in the original connected-account context, specifying exact amount R. Use `refund_application_fee=false` and do not request transfer reversal. Independently issue exact fee refund Q against the platform-owned original `ApplicationFee` using the platform account context. That refund credits the connected account, not the customer. Customer refund R must already include the refundable platform-fee share. [Direct-charge refund behavior](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/direct-charges?platform=web&ui=elements), [application-fee refund API](https://docs.stripe.com/api/fee_refunds/create).

Do not use automatic `refund_application_fee=true` as the canonical path: Stripe's charge-proportional rounding can differ from original unit allocations, especially if E is retained or taxes/pickup have different eligibility. Explicit Q preserves the required cents. Application fees can appear asynchronously after capture, so missing `fee_...` identity means pending resolution, not zero application fee. Resolve from original charge and application-fee observations before the fee-refund leg; correlate all platform fee objects to account/charge/mode. No refund may reference an unrelated fee.

**Amounts and policy:** R comprises the selected original units' discounted paid merchandise, eligible original item tax, refundable pickup/shipping/tax, P shares, legally required E shares and corresponding fee tax. Q equals the selected refundable original P shares, independent of R/T. Full legally required cancellation refunds all remaining customer components and all remaining P. Voluntary-return retention of E requires an approved cause-based policy and the correct tax adjustment. Default to returning E until that policy is approved. Unused shipping/fee shares remain independently refundable so an earlier item-only refund cannot consume the ability to refund them later. Quantity refunds select immutable unit ordinals. Store every component's remaining/refunded/reserved amounts, including those deferred after a unit merchandise refund.

| Leg | Local state and settlement evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Customer refund | `INTENDED`, `SUBMITTED`, `PENDING`, `SUCCEEDED`, `FAILED`, `CANCELED`, `UNKNOWN`/`RECOVERY_REQUIRED`; provider refund ID, amount, status, original charge and balance effects. Only verified success produces native settled refund allocations. |
| Application-fee refund | `INTENDED`, `WAITING_FOR_FEE`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNKNOWN`, `SUCCEEDED`, `FAILED`/`RECOVERY_REQUIRED`; exact Q, original fee, fee-refund ID and platform/connected balance evidence. Do not invent a Stripe `fee_refund.status`; local uncertainty is resolved by retrieving the fee-refund object and amount. |
| Combined operation | Track customer completion separately from financial reconciliation. Parent can report "Customer refunded; platform-fee reconciliation pending". Final financial completion requires both legs and component caps to reconcile. |

Reserve R and Q in one short transaction under deterministic parent/purchase/payment/component locks before external work. Enforce original-charge and original-application-fee caps including successful, submitted, pending and unknown operations; distinct vendor requests may run after safe reservation. Reject overlapping unit merchandise requests. Refundable fee portions need amount reservations, not the existing unique `platformChargeId` whole-object lock. Derive permitted P/E shares on the server for authorized vendor returns; do not let vendors select arbitrary platform charge IDs or grant them cross-vendor control. Persist the request hash and both operation keys. Refund-only platform components need a narrowly privileged path with the same original-share caps.

Submit both reserved legs through durable work, normally customer refund first and fee credit promptly after provider acceptance, including pending card refunds when the fee credit can help restore merchant balance. A platform application-fee refund returns only its own fee; it does not advance customer principal. If either result is uncertain, retrieve before retrying. If customer success precedes fee-refund failure, keep R settled and retry/reconcile Q without repeating R. If Q succeeds while R remains pending or fails, retain the fee credit as an observed financial movement, keep customer liability open and do not refund Q again. A replacement customer attempt after conclusive failure references the same component entitlement and previously returned Q. Failed/canceled refunds require reconciled reversal evidence before releasing or reusing their R reservation. Do not automatically re-charge a merchant to undo a returned application fee.

Keep existing loyalty/physical consequences tied to verified customer unit refund success, not delayed internal fee-credit completion. Extend `RefundWriterService`/refund states so existing hooks that require settled customer refunds still run exactly once while financial reconciliation can remain pending. Arbitrary fee-only refunds must not cancel physical units or restore points twice. Promotional discounts refund only amounts actually paid; reimbursements of platform-funded promotions are separate approved ledger movements. Refund original taxes according to legal eligibility; never refund a gross pre-discount item or current tax estimate. No-fee-taxes and refund-shipping booleans in current policies need versioned cause/component support before broader policies are enabled.

**Insufficient balance or restriction:** Card refunds may stay pending on the merchant account; refund creation does not prove customer receipt. Return P through its own leg, refresh status, notify the merchant of balance funding/remediation and keep the customer accurately informed. Do not top up from platform principal, reverse a transfer that does not exist, change original account context or mark a pending refund settled. Where API access is lost, route to vendor Dashboard/reconnection and monitored Stripe-assisted resolution. Legally required refunds remain due; use a documented merchant-funded alternative only with authorized evidence and reconciliation preventing duplicate reimbursement. Stripe generally retains original processing fees; vendor agreements and incident support must address this operational reality. [Refund balances, pending and failed outcomes](https://docs.stripe.com/refunds).

Dashboard-originated refunds/disputes must be ingested and reserve/reconcile remaining charge and fee capacity before another local refund. If an external arbitrary amount has no unambiguous original-unit mapping, quarantine the discrepancy for authorized allocation rather than guess quantities or award/restore loyalty. An external full refund requires reconciliation of remaining P; partial unclassified refunds need operator-reviewed fee allocation. Dispute principal, actual dispute fees and application-fee treatment are separately observed; prevent concurrent refund/dispute double reimbursement and assign merchant evidence deadlines. Returning P on a dispute is a separate policy decision, not an assumption that Stripe automatically returns it.

### Financial examples for direct charges

### Booth invoice architecture recommendation

Keep local `MarketInvoice` as authoritative invoice and automatic draft-generation/revision machinery. Recommend a **custom direct-charge collection flow on the market manager organization's account**, using the existing invoice provider seam and shared operation/refund engine. The vendor paying an invoice is the customer; the manager is the merchant. Do not route the payment to the payer vendor's Stripe account. Manager pays actual C, handles refunds/disputes and funds negative balances under the approved account model. Collect a separately displayed Service fee `E+P+H`; application fee is P only.

| Choice | Compatibility and cost |
| --- | --- |
| Stripe Invoicing with direct charges | Supported: create invoice and customer in the manager account and collect application fees. Preserve partial electronic payments as explicit amounts; every installment gets its own PI, fee snapshot and original line/component allocation. Do not mutate an issued invoice's original snapshot to add fees or reduce history; use collection snapshots and approved receipt/adjustment entries. Preserve revision increments and existing unpaid-only void/reissue rules unless an explicitly scoped extension is qualified.

Refund each collection against the original manager charge. Allocate rental/line tax and P/E/H at collection time, refund exact portions, and report refunded versus gross paid principal separately; define whether principal refund reopens amount due or credits/cancels the obligation according to reason. Payer identity/access, receipt issuer, invoice legal numbering and refund/reissue accounting require owner/counsel approval. Guest invoice links need scoped, expiring, non-enumerable authorization if approved, not fake vendor membership. Shared direct-charge refund and reconciliation safeguards are prerequisites for online invoice activation.

### Remaining blockers and production requirements

1. **Stripe/commercial:** Confirm eligibility for Stripe-owned fee/loss collection and full Dashboard merchant accounts for the actual marketplace and manager model, including existing OAuth accounts. Verify agreements, territories, application-fee collection/refunds, dispute costs and access after deauthorization. No silent fallback to platform liability.
2. **Legal/network/Stripe:** Approve exact Preorder/Service fee economics and disclosures across methods, including debit/prepaid, and fee tax treatment. Set cause-based E retention only where permitted; otherwise return it. Establish merchant-funded legally required refund escalation when normal card refund cannot complete.
3. **Owner/accounting:** Set platform fee rates and bases, processing estimate assumptions, rounding, geography/currency, fee tax owner, eligible shipping refunds, treatment of subsidies and returned promotion/loyalty value. Accept that failed aggregate checkout can still cost captured vendors nonrefundable processing fees. This cost cannot be removed by an atomicity claim.
4. **Technical acceptance:** Qualify installed Vendure multi-payment amount/transition hooks and legacy consumers, short bounded reservation leases, payment-method cloning and per-merchant authentication, pinned Stripe API/SDK/Accounts v2 event compatibility, exact refund status semantics and recovery beyond the idempotency window.
5. **Operations:** Decide customer-action timeout, finalization margin and merchant refund-response deadlines. Assign dispute evidence and pending-refund operators, monitoring and support messaging. Returning the application fee is mandatory; advancing customer principal from the platform is not a designed recovery mechanism.
6. **Booth invoices:** Approve custom collection against the local invoice, payer access, partial electronic fee policy, legal issuer and whether principal refunds reopen debt or issue credits. Preserve existing automatic generation/manual payments until the separate invoice gate passes.

The chosen direct-charge and refundable-platform-fee business direction is settled. The blockers above constrain safe implementation and activation; they do not authorize implementation, migrations, Stripe setup or a real-money trial in this planning phase.
